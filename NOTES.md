## Candidates (all 80–140 chars, two beats: setup → reframe → kicker)

1. (126 chars) "I spent the whole call on mute, then unmuted to say 'sorry, I was on mute' — the most honest thing I've said at work all year."
2. (125 chars) "I wrote 'per my last email' and attached the email, then attached a calendar invite to discuss why I had to attach the email."
3. (125 chars) "In standup I said 'no blockers' while blocked on three things, because honesty takes longer than the fifteen minutes we have."
4. (133 chars) "My boss sent a 'quick question' at 4pm, and three hours later the question has a project plan and I have a new job title: the answer."
5. (110 chars) "I said I'd circle back on Friday, and now it's Tuesday and I'm still circling — HR calls it a performance lap."
6. (130 chars) "The VPN drops every time I open a spreadsheet, so IT told me to stop opening spreadsheets and start opening tickets about the VPN."
7. (127 chars) "My badge opens every door except the one to the meeting I'm actually invited to, so I just stand outside nodding like I belong."
8. (125 chars) "IT reset my password to something secure, so now I write it on a sticky note and stick it to the thing the password protects."

## Head-to-head — why #1 wins

- #1 vs #2: #2's kicker repeats "attach the email" and lands on bureaucracy; #1 reframes a universal phrase ("sorry, I was on mute") as honesty — a sharper, fresher turn.
- #1 vs #3: #3 needs daily-standup context (niche); mute is universal to anyone who has ever been on a video call.
- #1 vs #4: #4's kicker ("new job title: the answer") is abstract wordplay; #1's kicker is concrete and self-deprecating.
- #1 vs #5: #5 has only one real beat; "performance lap" is a decent kicker but the setup does not earn it.
- #1 vs #6: #6 is a complaint loop whose kicker restates the complaint; #1 surprises by making the apology the honest part.
- #1 vs #7: #7's kicker ("nodding like I belong") is visual but has no reframe; #1 turns the meaning of its own quoted line.
- #1 vs #8: #8 is strong, but the password-on-a-sticky-note joke is a well-worn office gag; the mute line feels newer.

## Final pick

Candidate #1. 126 characters, 128 bytes UTF-8, single line, no trailing newline.
Mechanism: the mute button — unused by r1 and all neighbours. Two beats
(spent the call muted → unmuted only to apologise for being muted) plus a
kicker that reframes the apology as the year's most honest statement.
